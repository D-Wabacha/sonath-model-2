Do you need a website if you already have Instagram?

Sometimes no. Here is an honest way to decide, from people who build websites for a living.

4 min read  ·  Updated 16 October 2026  ·  By Sonath

Plenty of Kenyan businesses do well on Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp alone. If yours is one of them, a website will not change much. For others, it is the missing piece.

When Instagram is enough

  • Your customers already follow you, and most sales come from people who know you.
  • What you sell is visual and changes often: food, fashion, beauty, events.
  • You answer DMs quickly, and that is how orders happen.
  • Nobody asks you for a website, a price list or a company profile.

If all four are true, spend the money on better photos or ads instead.

When a website starts to matter

  • People search for what you do. Someone typing "conference venue in Nyeri" or "school in Kasarani" into Google sees websites and Google listings, not Instagram posts.
  • Bigger clients check you out. Companies, NGOs and government buyers often want a website and an email address on your own domain before they take you seriously.
  • The same questions keep coming. Prices, location, hours, fees, room types. A page answers them once, for everyone.
  • You do not own your Instagram. Accounts get hacked, suspended or buried by a change in what the app shows. A website and a domain are yours.
  • You take bookings or orders. A form that collects dates, names and details is easier for both sides than a long DM thread.

The middle ground

You do not have to choose between nothing and a big website. A sensible first step for many businesses is:

  1. A free Google Business Profile, so you show up on Google Maps with your hours, photos and phone number.
  2. A one-page website on your own domain, linked from your Instagram bio, with your services, prices, location and a WhatsApp button.

That covers search, trust and the repeated questions, and you keep Instagram for what it is good at. Our Launch page package is built for exactly this.

Quick check: search Google for what you sell plus your town. If competitors appear and you do not, that is the case for a website.
