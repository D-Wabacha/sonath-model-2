What to prepare before you talk to a developer

Half an hour of preparation gets you a better quote, a faster project and fewer surprises. Here is the checklist we would want from you.

5 min read  ·  Updated 16 October 2026  ·  By Sonath

You do not need to know anything technical. You need to know your business, and to have a few things ready to share.

1. One sentence on what the site or system is for

Finish this sentence: "When it is done, I want people to be able to…". For example: "…see our rooms and send a booking request" or "…record every sale so I know what stock is left." This one sentence shapes everything else.

2. Who will use it

Customers on their phones? Parents? Your own staff at the counter? Write down who they are and what they usually ask you. Those questions become the pages.

3. Three sites or apps you like

They do not have to be in your industry. Note what you like about each: the colours, how easy it is to book, how little text there is. This saves hours of guessing.

4. Your content, or a plan for it

  • Text: what you do, prices, opening hours, location, policies. Rough notes are fine.
  • Photos: real photos of your place, products and team beat stock photos every time.
  • Logo: the original file if you have it, not a screenshot.
  • Spreadsheets: for a system, the sheets or exercise books you use today.

5. Your domain and accounts

If you already have a domain, find out where it is registered and who has the login. If an old developer holds it, ask for it now. It can take a while.

6. A budget range

It can feel risky to say a number first. But a range like "under 100,000" or "up to 400,000" lets a developer tell you what is realistic, instead of guessing and quoting something you cannot use.

7. One person who decides

Projects slow down most when nobody can approve things. Pick one person who can answer questions and say yes each week.

Warning signs in a quote: no written scope, the domain registered in the developer's name, no mention of handing over the code, or full payment before any work is shown.

Questions to ask them

  1. Can I see two sites you built that are live right now?
  2. Who exactly will do the work?
  3. How often will I see progress?
  4. What do I own at the end?
  5. What happens if something breaks after launch?

We answer all five on our process page, so you can hold us to the same standard.
