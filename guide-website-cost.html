How much does a website cost in Kenya?

Quotes run from a few thousand shillings to several hundred thousand. Here is what makes the difference, what cheap quotes tend to leave out, and what to ask before you pay anything.

6 min read  ·  Updated 16 October 2026  ·  By Sonath

Ask five developers in Nairobi what a website costs and you can get five answers that differ by ten times. They are usually not quoting for the same thing.

What actually drives the price

The number of pages matters less than most people think. These matter more:

  • Template or custom design. A bought template, lightly edited, is fast and cheap. A design made for your business takes longer, but it fits your content and does not look like a thousand other sites.
  • Who writes the text. Good wording is often the slowest part of a website. If you supply finished text and photos, the job is smaller.
  • What the site has to do. A site that shows information is one job. A site that takes bookings, payments or orders is a bigger one, because it has to store data, send alerts and keep working when something goes wrong.
  • Payments. Taking M-Pesa or card payments means connecting to a payment provider, testing every case, and handling refunds and failed payments.
  • Languages. A Swahili version of every page is extra writing, checking and layout work.

What a cheap quote often leaves out

A low price is not a problem in itself. The problem is finding out later what was not included. Check for these:

  • Who owns the domain. If the developer registers your domain in their own name, they control your address. Ask for it to be registered in your name or your business's name.
  • Who owns the code and logins. Can you take the site to another developer next year? Ask for the source code and every login at handover.
  • Hosting after year one. Some quotes include hosting for one year and then charge whatever they like to renew.
  • Changes after launch. Is fixing a fault in the first month free? What does a small text change cost after that?
  • Phones. Most of your visitors will be on a phone. Ask to see a site the developer built, on your own phone, before you sign.

A simple test: ask the developer to show you a live site they built, and to tell you who owns that site's domain. A straight answer to both is a good sign.

The yearly costs

Once a site is live, there are a few running costs. For a small site they are usually modest:

  • Domain name. A .co.ke or .com address is renewed every year. It is usually a few thousand shillings a year.
  • Hosting. For a simple site this can be very cheap. Sites with bookings, payments or a database cost more to host.
  • Care. Backups, security updates and small changes. You can do this yourself, or pay someone monthly.

Five questions to ask before you pay

  1. Is this a fixed price, and what exactly does it include?
  2. Will the domain, hosting and code be in my name?
  3. How many rounds of changes are included?
  4. What happens, and what does it cost, if something breaks after launch?
  5. How will I pay, and in how many stages?

If you get clear answers in writing, you can compare quotes fairly, whatever the price.
